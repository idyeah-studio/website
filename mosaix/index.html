Employee onboarding · the concept

Your butterfly, filling up.

When you join Mosaix, this little butterfly is already your logo. We wanted it to mean something more. So we made it yours to fill.

Just arrived

Fully you

Most onboarding is boring. It is a list of things someone tells you to finish, and then it stops after the first week.

We wanted the opposite. A six month story where you can actually see yourself growing. Every time you settle in, learn something, or meet someone, a little more colour fills in.

Day one, the butterfly is mostly waiting for you. By day 180, it is fully you. That is the whole idea, and everything else is built around it.

How it's built

A calm home, not a busy dashboard. During onboarding, the new hire only sees what they need. Everything else in Mosaix waits for later.

🪞 Mosaix
Today My Journey Learning opens later My Skills Assessment opens later
Ask Benny Updates Help Account
A short, focused menu

Today is your daily home and My Journey is the whole six months. Learning, My Skills and Assessment switch on as you reach them. Everything else in Mosaix waits until you have settled in.

No busy top bar

No search, no switcher, no rows of icons. If you want to find something, you just ask Benny. Notes and reminders live quietly under Updates.

Tap, don't type

Wherever we can, you tap, pick, or slide instead of typing. And there is always a kind word, even on a slow week. Falling behind should feel held, not judged.

The wireframes

Grey boxes, no colour yet, but every region labelled and every choice explained. Each screen a new hire touches is here, whether or not it sits in the menu.

What this deck covers

The full employee onboarding experience, every employee story in the requirements: pre-board, your journey, induction, compliance, people, buddy, the learning plan, your skills, the six-month assessment, updates, and the hand-off into your development plan.

What it does not cover, on purpose

The manager, HR admin and BU head views. Those are different people with different needs, and they get their own decks. This one stays about the new hire.

Privacy (US 9.1): a new hire only ever sees their own journey and their own assessment. Nothing about anyone else.
